THE CHALLENGE
- Pick one real problem that you have faced on an Indian public-service website or digital service.
- Build a simpler, clearer and more useful way to solve that problem.
- Possible areas include:
  - travel
  - taxes
  - pensions
  - certificates
  - payments
  - grievances
  - other public needs
- IRCTC, EPFO and the Income Tax portal are examples only; they are not a fixed list.

WHAT THE ORGANIZERS WANT YOU TO BUILD
- The prototype for this hackathon should be built with Codex or powered by an OpenAI model.
- Codex should be a meaningful part of the build process; it should not be added only for the submission.
- If a build is selected among the 10 finalists, OpenAI will provide support.
- The prototype should solve the real problem through a complete citizen journey.
- Use mock data, mock accounts and mock backend behavior where production access would be unsafe or unavailable.
- Reviewers will test the citizen experience rather than an admin panel.

Prototype requirements:
- Solve one clearly defined user problem.
- Allow the main journey to be completed from start to finish.
- Be easier to understand or use than the current experience.
- Be designed for real Indian users, including:
  - people using mobile devices
  - people on slower connections
  - people with limited digital experience
- Use mock or synthetic data wherever personal information, payments, OTPs or government systems would normally be involved.

WHAT A STRONG BUILD LOOKS LIKE
A strong submission makes the following points obvious:
- Who is facing the problem?
- What is difficult about the current experience?
- What did you change?
- Why is your version better?
- What works today, and what is still mocked?
- How could the idea work safely at a larger scale?

Experience/build expectation:
- Show the complete citizen journey.
- A static design by itself is not enough.
- The interface and interactions should work from start to finish.
- Mocked data and dependencies should be clearly identified.

WHAT NOT TO DO
The site says not to:
- Try to access, test or interfere with a live government system.
- Reverse-engineer private systems or use undocumented private APIs.
- Scrape personal or restricted information.
- Use real Aadhaar numbers.
- Use real PAN details.
- Use real passwords.
- Use real OTPs.
- Use real payment details.
- Use real health information.
- Use other sensitive data.
- Present the prototype as an official government product.
- Use government logos in a way that suggests approval or partnership.
- Submit an old project with only small changes.
- Include code, assets or data that you do not have permission to use.

WHAT TO SUBMIT
Submission deadline:
- August 28, 2026 at 8:00 PM IST.
- There is no grace period after the form closes.

Required submission items:
1. Live public link
   - Must open in a browser without requesting access.
   - Reviewers will not download a mobile app.
   - Include mock consumer login credentials if the project requires them.

2. One video
   - Maximum length: two minutes.
   - First minute: demo the project as a citizen.
   - Second minute: explain how you built it and why you made those choices.
   - Both teammates may present.

3. Project summary
   - Under 250 words.
   - Explain what the project is.
   - Explain why it is better than the current solution.

4. Partner's registered email
   - Required if the entrant is a team of two.
   - Leave blank when participating solo.
   - Both teammates must register.
   - Each teammate must submit the other teammate's registered email.

General link requirement:
- Every submitted link must work without requesting access.

HOW BUILDS ARE JUDGED
Judging dimensions and questions:

Problem
- Is this a real and important user problem?

Working build
- Does the main journey actually work?

Usability
- Is the experience simpler, clearer and more accessible?

Product thinking
- Are the choices thoughtful and well explained?

End-to-end thinking
- Does the solution address backend, infrastructure and processes, rather than only the interface?

Honesty
- Are limitations, mock data and dependencies clearly disclosed?

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT
- Selection takes place in two stages after the form closes.
- The same email address must be used at every step.
- The email is how the organizers track an entry.
- An entry cannot be moved to another email address.

STAGE 1 — SHORTLIST OF 250
- Every submission received by August 28, 2026 at 8:00 PM IST is reviewed by the team together with OpenAI.
- Review period: August 28 through September 1.
- The top 250 builds are shortlisted.
- Everyone who submitted receives an email with the result.
- Not being in the 250 does not mean the build was bad; the site says the field is large and competition is strong.

STAGE 2 — 250 TO 10
- The 250 shortlisted participants get one week of mentorship.
- Mentorship is provided through a WhatsApp group.
- The group has five mentors from engineering, tech and the OpenAI team.
- The goal of mentorship is to tighten/improve the project.
- After mentorship, participants resubmit the improved build.
- Resubmission uses the same format.
- Resubmission deadline: September 7, 2026.
- Resubmission uses the same email addresses as before.
- The 10 finalists are announced between September 8 and September 12, 2026.
- The top 250 are honoured on a public page.
- Finalists present live in Bengaluru on September 12, 2026.
- The live presentation is to:
  - founders
  - creators
  - the mentors
  - invited government officials
- Winners are announced the same day.
- Selection does not guarantee that a government body will adopt or implement a build.
- The stated purpose of the initiative is to start a useful conversation about better public digital experiences.

PRIZES
- Top 10:
  - one year of Codex Pro
  - a Codex Micro
- Top 3:
  - a MacBook
  - in addition to the Top 10 prizes
- Winner:
  - a trip to San Francisco, subject to visa
  - plus all of the prizes above

Builder Brief closing line/theme:
- Choose the problem.
- Build the solution.
- Show what better looks like.

REGISTERING

Does filling out the registration form confirm my place?
- No.
- The registration form is an expression of interest.
- Updates will be shared by email and WhatsApp.

Do I need to be a developer?
- No.
- Developers, designers, product thinkers, students and independent builders can apply.
- Applicants should be able to frame the problem and build the prototype themselves.

Can I participate alone or as a team?
- Participation can be solo or in a team of two.
- Both teammates must register separately.
- Each teammate must use their own email address.
- Each teammate must enter the other person's registered email in the Project Submission form.
- Participants must be 18 or older.

SCOPE OF THE BUILD

What exactly should I build?
- Choose one specific problem within a public-service website or digital journey, OR rethink the entire experience.
- Build a better solution.

Is a Figma design enough?
- No.
- Designs may be part of the submission.
- The main journey must be a working prototype.

Does every feature need to work?
- Every feature that is demoed must work.
- If presenting on stage, show the feature working rather than relying on an explanation.

TOOLS AND RULES

Is Codex mandatory?
- Yes, for the prototype submitted to the hackathon.
- Codex should be meaningfully involved in the build.
- Other development tools and libraries are allowed.
- The submission should explain how Codex contributed.
- If a public-service body later chooses to develop the solution further, there are no restrictions from this hackathon on the technology used for that later implementation.
- The builder and the public-service body can decide what technology best fits the project.
- If a build is selected among the 10 finalists, OpenAI will provide support.

Can I use other tools or libraries?
- Yes.
- You must have the right to use them.
- You must disclose them in the submission.

Can I use an existing government website as a starting point?
- You can study an existing government site to understand the problem.
- You cannot copy its code.
- You cannot build on its infrastructure.
- You cannot reverse-engineer its private systems.
- The prototype should be an independent build.

Can I connect to a live government system?
- No, unless the organizers provide a specifically approved public sandbox.
- Use mock data and simulated integrations.

Can I use real user data?
- No.
- Use fictional or synthetic data.
- Never enter:
  - real IDs
  - passwords
  - OTPs
  - payment details
  - sensitive personal information

Can I use official logos?
- Do not use a logo in a way that makes the prototype appear official or endorsed.
- Clearly label the build as an independent hackathon prototype.

Can I submit something I already built?
- The submission should be made for this hackathon.
- Normal starter templates or open-source components may be used if their licences allow it.
- Such components should be clearly disclosed.

SUBMITTING AND JUDGING

When is the submission deadline?
- August 28, 2026 at 8:00 PM IST.
- The form closes at that time.
- There is no grace period.

What should the demo video show?
- Maximum two minutes.
- Minute one: demo the project as a citizen.
- Minute two: explain how it was built and why the builder made those choices.
- Loom, OBS or another public video link is acceptable.
- Both teammates may present.

What else does the submission form require?
- A live public browser link.
- A project summary under 250 words.
- The partner's registered email when the entrant has a teammate.
- Mock consumer credentials when the project requires a login.
- Never submit a real password or sensitive personal data.

Will visual design alone win?
- Good design matters, but design alone is not enough.
- Judges also examine:
  - whether the problem is real
  - whether the core journey works
  - whether the solution is practical

THE EVENT

Will the winning builds be adopted by the government?
- There is no promise of adoption.
- The goal is to surface strong ideas.
- The goal is also to start a constructive conversation about better public digital experiences.

Is this an official government hackathon?
- No.
- It is an independent builder initiative presented by Varun Mayya in partnership with OpenAI.
- Government or public-service representatives may be invited.
- Their participation or endorsement should not be assumed.

What happens if I am selected as a finalist?
- Finalists will be asked to prepare a short demo.
- Finalists take part in a filmed finale in Bengaluru on September 12.
- The finale is recorded.
- It is not live-streamed.
- Winners are announced the same day.
- The team will contact selected builders regarding:
  - technical checks
  - rehearsal
  - travel
  - accommodation
  - filming consent

When is the finale?
- Planned date: September 12, 2026.
- Location: Bengaluru.
- Exact timing is not specified on the public site.
- Exact venue is not specified on the public site.
- Preparation details will be shared directly with selected builders.

Are there prizes or Codex credits?
- All 10 finalists get:
  - one year of Codex Pro
  - a Codex Micro
- The top 3 also get:
  - a MacBook
- The top build wins:
  - a trip to San Francisco, subject to visa

YOUR DATA AND RIGHTS

Who can see my registration information?
- Registration and submission data will be managed by Varun Mayya's team.
- The OpenAI team will not have access to this data.
- The final privacy notice is expected to explain:
  - who can access the data
  - why the data is collected
  - when the data will be deleted

Who owns my build?
- Submit only work that you own or have permission to use.
- You retain full rights to the build.

Where will updates be shared?
- Through the email address used during registration.
- Through the WhatsApp number used during registration.
- Important dates and rule changes should also appear on the official website.
